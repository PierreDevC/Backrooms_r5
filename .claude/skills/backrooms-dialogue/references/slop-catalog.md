# Slop Catalog

<!-- Imported from Notion: https://app.notion.com/p/3ec79be4683a81f5aa48d5fa85c1fdb2 (snapshot 2026-10-01) -->

Use in step 5 of the skill. AI-written dialogue gives itself away at four levels: stock phrases, line shapes, story shapes and game-writing habits. Cleaning up the words isn't enough on its own. Research on AI fiction (StoryScope, 2026) found that how a story is built gives it away almost as reliably as its word choice. Check all four levels.

When a line hits a pattern, remove the move. Swapping in a synonym keeps the problem.

## 1. Stock phrases

| Pattern | Looks like | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Breath and whisper clichés | "voice barely above a whisper", "a breath she didn't know she was holding", "a shaky breath" | Cut it. The line carries its own volume, or the channel does (radio static, tape hiss). |
| Atmosphere filler | "the air grew heavy", "something shifted", "a chill ran down his spine", "an eerie silence" | Say what changed: the lights stopped buzzing, the carpet is wet here, the jazz got closer. |
| Smell on autopilot | ozone, copper, "the smell of decay" | Only when the smell matters to play or is strange in a specific way. |
| Inflated words | testament, tapestry, palpable, unspoken, delve, "a dance of", "echoes of", and "liminal" spoken in-world | Plain words. Explorers don't say "liminal". |
| Stock tension lines | "We need to talk." "Something's not right." "We don't have much time." "It's not that simple." | The specific version: "Batteries are on one bar and you're humming again." |
| AI-favorite names | Elara, Kael, Lyra, Thorne, Voss, Seraphina, Elian, Dr. Aris | Names that sit with the existing cast (Marsh, Okafor, Reyes, Brandt, Hale). Any new name is new canon. |

## 2. Line shapes

| Pattern | Looks like | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Negation pivot | "It's not a hallway. It's a throat." or "not because X, but because Y" | Say the second half plainly, or say nothing. |
| Rule of three | "cold, quiet and wrong" | Pick the one that matters. |
| Fragment stacks | "Dark. Silent. Waiting." | One fragment is a choice. Three in a row is a tic. |
| Aphorism closer | "Some doors are meant to stay closed." | End on something concrete, or mid-thought. |
| Echo and confirm | "The code?" "The code." more than once in a scene | Let the other speaker move the scene forward. |
| Perfect answers | Every question answered fully, in order, politely | Dodge, answer a different question, answer late, or don't answer. |
| Therapy speak under threat | "I need you to hear me." "That's valid." | Blunt, wrong or quiet. |
| Name in every line | "Listen, Reyes. Reyes, look at me." | Names only to get attention, or when one slips out under stress. |
| Punctuation drama | Em dashes and ellipses in every line | One per scene at most in written text. None in voiced lines. |
| Matching voices | Everyone shares sentence length, vocabulary and politeness | Use the voice sheets and run the swap test. |

## 3. Story shapes

| Pattern | Looks like | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Stated theme | A character or note explains what it all means: memory, regret, being lost | Cut it. Let objects carry it: a drawing, a tape, an empty bed. |
| Body-and-weather emotion | Fear is always a tight chest or cold sweat, and the setting always mirrors the mood | Mix in plain statements and contradictions: a bad joke, an argument about batteries. |
| Tidy causality | Every tape confirms the last, every mystery gets answered | Let accounts disagree. Let a tape end before it gets to the point. |
| One track | Every line serves the current objective | Let a line touch something outside it that never resolves. Flag it as new canon. |
| Philosophy talk | Characters debate what reality is or what the place wants | They're trying to survive. They talk logistics, blame, food, batteries, who's slowing who down. |
| Clean acceptance | Inner peace, closure, a lesson learned | Endings can be partial, tired or uneasy unless Pierre's design says otherwise. |
| Vague allusion | "that place", "the thing", "what happened back there" | Name it the way the speaker would. Withhold the explanation, keep the detail: "It had Brandt's voice. It said my name wrong." |

## 4. Game-writing habits

| Pattern | Looks like | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Narrated directives | "I need to find a way through that door." | Let objective text do it, or give the speaker a reason: "Housekeeping keeps spares somewhere. They always do." |
| Repeated quest info | The same name, place or code said three times in one exchange | Once, clearly. Then move on. |
| Mechanics in a character's mouth | "Staring at the wallpaper drains your sanity." | Briefings and the UI own mechanics. Dialogue owns the fear: "Don't look at the walls too long. I mean it." |
| Talking to the player as an audience | "Welcome, wanderer." "You there, listen carefully." | Speakers talk to whoever they think is on the channel, or to themselves. |
| Filler choices | "Tell me more." "What do you think?" | Only relevant if dialogue choices are added. See channels-and-barks.md. |

## Fast pass

Before delivering, every answer should be yes:

- Could each line only come from this speaker, at this moment?
- Is each must-know fact said once, clearly?
- Does each exchange end on something concrete or unfinished rather than a summary?
- Would the lines hold up read aloud by someone tired and scared?
- Is the punctuation in voiced lines plain enough for TTS and speech recognition?
