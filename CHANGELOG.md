# CHANGELOG

## r6 — 2026-10-01 · Story: new places, branching objectives and rewritten dialogue in every level
Written with the `backrooms-dialogue` skill (imported from Notion into `.claude/skills/`). Full design, every new line, new canon and proposed voice sheets: `docs/STORY.md`. All new lines are subtitles (text only).
- **Shared story layer** (`src/story.js`): per-level objectives and optional leads, a paper document overlay that doesn't pause the tape (E closes it), **FIELD NOTES** (J, or pause → FIELD NOTES) listing objectives, leads and every document found (re-readable), hold-to-act interactions with progress in the prompt, and run flags that carry choices from level to level.
- **Level 0** (`src/places0.js`): three rooms carved into the maze with real doorways (M.E.G. BASE CAMP, SUBSTATION 4B, FLOODED OFFICE), spray-painted M.E.G. chevrons to camp and substation. The exit keypad now needs **power**: three loud breakers in the substation (the Howlers come to look) **or** Brandt's battery carried from camp (no sprinting) and connected at the door. Leads: Okafor's logbook (three pages, a spare battery), Reyes hurt in the flooded office (give her water for her med kit and a Mimic warning), the camp radio to Outpost Nine (locks the tape signal), whiteboard, hand-drawn map, supply crate. Explorers react to each other's deaths, to the breakers and to your choices; the Mimic borrows Reyes's voice once you've helped her. Checkpoints BATTERY, POWER ON.
- **Level 9** (`src/places9.js`): street signs and names, house numbers. The Hale house on Maple Street (kitchen notes, photographs, a wall safe), the blue house (the relief team, Abara's tape, a torn page, a **fifth canister locker**: any four of five), the block captain's house (patrol rota: the HUD then times the next patrol; meeting minutes). **Second road to the elevator:** Hale's spare keycard in his safe (combination in his lab notes, or his kitchen note plus the nameplate); curing him is now a choice. Rewritten arrival radio that remembers Level 0. Checkpoint SPARE KEYCARD.
- **Level 5** (`src/places5.js`): the night manager's office (switchboard, night audit), the guest register, the rye on the Beverly bar, and a guest who never checked out behind a locked door. **Second road through the staff door:** his master key, in exchange for the rye. His room can be opened afterwards (it's empty). All Outpost Nine lines rewritten; a cured Hale speaks from the elevator, leaves in it, and takes some radio lines; hotel-specific whispers. Checkpoint MASTER KEY.
- **Level 18** (`src/places18.js`): the ART ROOM (bright and safe), the **crayons needed to colour in the door** (pick them up any time; otherwise the door waits after the fourth pin and you colour it in), an optional **fifth drawing** (Dad's camcorder), a LOST & FOUND listing objects from your earlier choices, the Children's letter on paper in their own spelling, rewritten memory captions and whispers, and a **YOU REMEMBERED card assembled from the whole run** (Hale, the explorers lost, Reyes, the guest's room, the camera). Checkpoint CRAYONS.
- **Level 9 terminals** (`src/hack9.js`): downloading is now **PACKET STACK**, a small falling-block puzzle on the terminal's own screen. Drop blocks into a 6×9 well and complete rows; each row verifies a packet (3 / 4 / 5 per terminal by difficulty) and moves the transfer. Fill the well to the top and the link corrupts (one packet lost, the modem screeches, a sleeping Wretch may stir). A/D or ←/→ move, W/↑ turns, S/↓ drops faster, Space slams, E steps back (RESUME at the terminal); on phones the stick moves, turns and drops. The camcorder pushes in on the screen while you play (up to 3.6×), a hunting Wretch knocks you off the keys, and toasts and subtitles move out of the way.
- **Level 9 windows you can see through** (`src/windows9.js`): enterable ground floors have real openings (sill and head stay solid, so collision, light and line of sight are unchanged), faint glass and curtains drawn back. Upstairs rooms live off-map, so their windows hold portal panes: while you are up there and facing one, a second camera standing where the room physically is renders the street into it (true parallax; the facade's own window dressing is hidden from it). **LOOK OUTSIDE** at every real window puts the camcorder at the glass; any step or E lets go. Watch the street from behind a latched door.
- **Door peepholes** (V, or PEEP on phones; the door prompt shows [V] PEEPHOLE). Inspired by the peephole in *No, I'm Not a Human*: the camcorder goes to the far face of the closed door, looking away from you through a wide lens; a new Level 9 post pass bends it into a fisheye porthole, washes it teal like a cheap night lens, posterises it and drops the line count, and a porch light picks out whoever is standing outside. If something is within a few metres of the door the lens breathes and your heartbeat climbs. You can turn about 40° either way; V, E or a step lets go. Works on every Level 9 door, so you can latch a room and check the hall.
- **Deadbolts.** Every Level 9 door has a brass thumb-turn on both faces and a bolt: latching turns the thumb-turn a quarter and throws the bolt into the frame, with its own sound (ratchet clicks, then the bolt going home; drawing it back is lighter).
- Engine: `PropBatch.finish()` disposes the roots of merged parts, so story meshes that must persist (pages, breaker handles, signs, text planes) hang off a `twin()` root. Level 0 rooms have no free-standing pillars. Long toasts wrap; subtitles and toasts move aside while a document is open.
- QA: `qa_r6/l0_story.js`, `l9_story.js`, `l5_story.js`, `l18_story.js` (both routes where there are two). `qa5/run5.js` and `qa5/s9_to5.js` no longer hard-code sandbox paths. Older Level 18 tests pick up the crayons before pinning (`qa18/s18_*.js`, `qa44/cpl.js`).

## r5 — 2026-10-01 · Scanned surfaces, skinned & animated characters, scanned props
- **Photo-scanned PBR surfaces in all four levels.** 27 CC0 texture sets from ambientCG (albedo + normal + ambient occlusion / roughness / height). Surfaces either take the scan as their base (Level 9 asphalt, lawns, siding, roofs, wood floors, board fences, plaster; Level 0 ceiling tiles; hotel checker floor and boiler concrete; Level 18 turf and wood) or keep their hand-made pattern and gain the scan's relief as detail (Level 0 yellow wallpaper and the damp carpet stains, hotel wallpaper and carpet, Level 18 murals, tiles and carpets). New physically based highlight (GGX / Smith / Schlick) driven by the per-pixel roughness and AO, so wet asphalt, worn carpet and painted plaster react differently to the flashlight.
- **Real characters.** The M.E.G. explorers and the bodies in Level 0, the Howler, the Level 9 Watch, house Wretches, lab Subject and Dr. Hale, and Level 18's Forgotten are now one skinned, GPU-animated human (Quaternius Universal Base Characters) driven by 24 motion-captured clips (Quaternius Universal Animation Library 1 & 2): idle / walk / jog / sprint picked and rate-matched from their real ground speed, crouch, flashlight, interact, knock, push, hit, death, get-up, zombie gait and more, with crossfades. Each role keeps its silhouette through bone-proportion profiles (the Howler's long arms and neck, the Watch's height, the Forgotten's stretched limbs) and a procedurally painted outfit (hazmat suit, rags, hospital gown, lab coat, faceless skin). The AI still turns heads, aims flashlights and leans through the same rig interface. Smilers, the Crawler, the Mimic's tells, moths and the Plush Dino keep their own look.
- **Scanned props.** 17 CC0 Poly Haven models, simplified to 400–2,200 triangles with two small WebP maps each and merged per 28.8 m area into a handful of draw calls: Level 0 cardboard box stacks, wet-floor sign and the TV cart's CRT (the static screen sits on the curved glass); Level 9 sofas, armchairs, nightstands, CRT sets, storage shelving stocked with boxes, fire hydrants, trash cans and garden gnomes; Level 5 armchairs, nightstands, the lounge chesterfield, an abandoned suitcase, boiler-room crates and drums; Level 18 kid-size school chairs repainted in each seat's colour, the teacher's chair, a school desk and rubber ducks in the toy bins. Level generation, collision and interactions are unchanged: each level makes the same random draws with or without the models.
- **Movement and collision.** Player movement runs in substeps of at most 10 cm, so a sprint plus a knock-back at a low frame rate can no longer pop through a thin wall or prop. Walking enemies also move in wall-safe substeps and keep personal space: two Howlers or Forgotten converging on you no longer merge into one body (Smilers and moths are exempt).
- **Fallbacks.** Every asset is optional: `?noassets` (original procedural look everywhere), `?noskin` (procedural characters), `?nomodels` (procedural props). The level layout is identical in every mode (checked with the same seed: identical level structure and collision boxes in all four modes, except that Level 9's model-only garden gnomes add their own small footprints and Level 5's crates use the scanned crate's rotated footprint).
- **Size.** The single HTML grows from 4.67 MB to 12.84 MB: the processed assets are embedded as base64 in `game/src/assetpack.js` (8.1 MB, generated by `game/tools/build_assets.py`; only texture sets the code references are packed). Textures decode lazily per level.
- New modules `assetpack.js`, `assets.js`, `models.js` (after shaders.js) and `skin.js` (after actors.js). Asset tools in `game/tools/` (process_tex.py, build_assets.py, unpack_assets.py, chr/, mdl/). Credits and licences in `docs/CREDITS.md`; pipeline notes in `docs/ASSETS.md`. New QA in `qa_r5/` (rerun of the r4.4 suites, seeded before/after captures, prop and character staging, same-seed layout check of the fallback modes; results in docs/QA.md). `qa18/s18_more.js`: its last step looked for the r4 Continue Level 18 button removed in r4.1; it now checks SELECT LEVEL → Level 18.

## r4.4 — 2026-10-01 · Checkpoints, Level 9 in SELECT LEVEL, right-click latch, crash safety, more almond water
- **Checkpoints on Easy and Normal (all four levels).** Dying now offers ▶ RETRY FROM CHECKPOINT (focused, shows the checkpoint name) next to ◀◀ RESTART LEVEL N / ◀◀ START OVER. Retrying keeps everything you finished in that level (tapes and code digits, downloads, keys, valves, drawings, opened doors, carried items); you respawn at the checkpoint with HP 100 / 80, sanity at least 85 / 70, battery at least 35 and a short fade-in, and the level's monsters are sent back to sleep or far away. A small amber "◆ CHECKPOINT · label" note appears under the camcorder readout when one is saved. Nightmare is unchanged: death restarts the level.
- Checkpoints: Level 0 level start and each TAPE n/4; Level 9 level start, M.E.G. MAP, each M.E.G. DATA n/3 (you respawn on that house's front porch, outside, and the downloaded terminal stays done), THE LAB, CROWBAR, CANISTER n/4, LURE PRIMED, SUBJECT CAGED, KEYCARD; Level 5 level start, KEY n/3, STAFF DOOR, BOILER ROOM, VALVE n/3; Level 18 level start, SUNSHINE ROOM, DRAWING n/4, PINNED n/4. Checkpoints last for the current run of a level; they are not saved across page reloads or level switches.
- **Level 9 is in SELECT LEVEL** (order 0 · 9 · 5 · 18, "LEVEL 9 · DARKENED SUBURBS"), with fresh supplies on the current difficulty; the first-arrival Level 9 briefing still applies.
- **Right-click latches doors.** Aim at a door and right-click to latch / unlatch it (prompt "[E] OPEN DOOR   [RIGHT-CLICK] LATCH"). Right-click elsewhere still holds zoom; X still latches; the phone LATCH button is unchanged. The Level 9 briefing (slide 3 picture inset and key text), the arrival toast and the controls panel were updated. The death screen's restart button is now "◀◀ RESTART LEVEL N" (the old ↻ glyph could show as a box).
- **Crash at the third computer (crouch + Wretch chase in a house).** No in-game exception reproduced in scripted or real-frame headless runs of that situation. The most likely cause was the old Ctrl-crouch: Ctrl held while pressing W is the browser's Ctrl+W "close tab" shortcut, which looks like a crash. Ctrl no longer crouches (C toggles crouch), and closing or reloading the tab mid-level now asks for confirmation. The frame loop was also hardened: simulation, world effects, rendering and timers are wrapped so one bad frame is logged ("[recovered …]") instead of freezing the game.
- **More almond water on Easy / Normal.** Placed water Easy / Normal / Nightmare: Level 0 14 / 9 / 5, Level 9 15 / 10 / 6, Level 5 12 / 8 / 5, Level 18 10 / 7 / 3. Fresh-start water: Easy 3, Normal 1, Nightmare 0 (Level 5/18 fresh starts keep 1 on Nightmare). Was (r4.3): Level 0 9 / 7 / 5, Level 9 10 / 8 / 6, Level 5 8 / 6 / 5, Level 18 6 / 5 / 3, Easy start 2. Nightmare placement unchanged.
- Layout: on short landscape screens (height ≤ 500 px) the death screen compacts and puts its buttons in one row so MAIN MENU stays visible; on narrow portrait screens long toasts (e.g. "M.E.G. DATA 1/3 RECEIVED") wrap instead of running off both edges. The controls panel reads "RMB/X Latch door".
- New module src/checkpoint.js (between brief.js and ui.js). New tests qa44/*.js with logs; qa18/s_levels.js now checks four options. Screenshots docs/screenshots/r4_4_*.jpg; brief/l9_s3.webp regenerated.

## r4.3 — 2026-10-01 · Level 9 hearing Wretches, noise meter and Level 9 briefing
- House Wretches are now blind and hunt by sound only. Entering a house, standing still, using the flashlight or being seen no longer wakes them. Noise only counts while you are inside that Wretch's house.
- Each Wretch builds suspicion from noise × distance. Above 30% it groans and stirs (one-time toast "SOMETHING HEARD YOU · STAY QUIET"); at 100% it wakes and runs to where it last heard you. If you go quiet, it loses you after 4 / 6 / 8 s (Easy / Normal / Nightmare) and goes back to prowling.
- Hearing range at full noise: 6.5 / 8.5 / 10 m (×1.2 when stirring, ×1.5 while chasing). Rough wake distances: walking ~2.7 / 3.6 / 4.2 m, sprinting up to the full range; crouch-walking is silent enough to pass at under 1 m.
- Player noise in Level 9 now scales with speed: crouch ~0.04, walk ~0.42, sprint 1.0; it fades in 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.1 s.
- New Level 9 NOISE meter in the camcorder HUD: segmented bar + SILENT / QUIET / NOISY / LOUD, an amber mark showing the nearest Wretch's hearing limit at your distance, and IT STIRS… / HEARD! / HUNTED states. One-time tip: "A WRETCH IS NEAR · KEEP YOUR NOISE LEFT OF THE MARK".
- Terminal downloads no longer randomly wake Wretches. The modem screech at the start and half-way is a loud noise pulse at the terminal (it can stir nearby Wretches; walking near them afterwards wakes them faster).
- New 4-slide LEVEL 9 BRIEFING with annotated in-game screenshots (YOUR OBJECTIVE, THE NEIGHBORHOOD WATCH, THE WRETCHES, STAY QUIET). It opens on first arrival in Level 9 (localStorage br_brief9) before the intro, and is available any time in Level 9 via pause → FIELD BRIEFING. Esc / SKIP enters the level. Controls field note updated.
- QA runners (qa/run.js, qa/run_m.js, qa5/run5.js, qa18/run18.js) pre-set br_brief9 so older scripts still start Level 9 directly; set BR_SHOW_BRIEF9=1 to keep the first-run briefing.
- Added qa9/hearing.js and qa9/brief9.js with logs; screenshots docs/screenshots/r4_3_*.jpg; slide art brief/l9_s1–4.webp. The street Watch and lab Subject are unchanged.
